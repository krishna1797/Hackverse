Architecture and decision logic

How the three agents combine

Three agents score the same stock independently and in parallel, each returning a signed score on a −100 to +100 scale plus its own confidence. A synthesis layer blends them with a deterministic risk-weighted linear model — no agent can act alone.

  • MomentumAgent — day move, volume against its own average, and six-session slope.
  • SentimentAgent — financial lexicon scan over headlines, scored for both mean tone and disagreement between headlines.
  • FilingsAgent — lexical TF-IDF retrieval over the filing corpus, hard-partitioned by issuer so a rival's filing can never be cited as this company's.

Each profile carries its own weights, action gates, conviction floor, and position cap. Weights are then tilted by the confidence each agent reported, so a hesitant agent moves the composite less than a certain one, and renormalised back to 1.

What happens when something breaks

  • Price feed down — momentum is dropped, its weight moves to the surviving agents, and conviction is cut 25%.
  • Filing missing or no relevant match — the fundamental leg is dropped rather than answered from a weak match, and conviction is cut 15%.
  • Agents disagree — when the active agents point in opposite directions, the conflict is stated on screen and conviction is cut 15% more.
  • Thin evidence — every profile has a conviction floor. Below it the call is to stand aside, on both the buy and the sell side.

Retrieval here is lexical TF-IDF, not embeddings. It runs with no network and no key, which is why it is used for the demo; the retrieval call is isolated behind one function so an embedding index would drop into the same interface.

All prices, filings, headlines and forward returns in this build are synthetic and held in memory. Nothing here is investment advice, and the output is a research simulation rather than a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
